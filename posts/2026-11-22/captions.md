## Carousel caption
Costco has charged $1.50 for a hot dog and soda since the 1980s 🌭

The deal loses money on paper, and the co-founder reportedly warned: "If you raise the effing hot dog, I will kill you. Figure it out."
Why hold the price? Because the real profit is the membership card: $5.3 billion in fees in fiscal 2025, about half of Costco's operating income.
Costco even built its own hot dog to protect the price: its own Kirkland franks since 2009, and cheaper drinks along the way.
One cheap product can sell the whole store.

## Reel script
McDonald's collected over ten billion dollars in rent in twenty twenty-four. Rent, not burgers. In nineteen fifty-six, the company set up a real estate arm to buy land and lease it to its franchisees. Today they pay McDonald's rent, plus royalties on their sales. That rent alone was nearly forty percent of the company's total revenue. McDonald's isn't just selling you fries. It's its franchisees' landlord.
